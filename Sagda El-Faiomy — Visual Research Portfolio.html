My research workflow

SourcePick a regionally significant but non-touristy subject: a municipal building, a university hall, a domestic film still. Use Wikimedia Commons, Creative Commons, public-domain collections or original photography.
Verify licenseRecord the license, author, URL and date checked before anything else happens.
Confirm identityCross-check with Google Search, Google Lens, TinEye and Arabic-language sources, so the ground truth does not rest on one page.
ObscureCrop or mask the signage, facade or title text. Keep the surroundings that a researcher could realistically follow.
Write and testDraft the prompt, rubric and notes, then attempt the task myself from the degraded image to confirm it needs multi-step research and has one defensible answer.

Worked examples: Port Said

Three complete evaluation instances: two from my own photographs and one from a CC BY image on Wikimedia Commons.

Instance 1: a bronze monument

Prompt: This photo shows a public monument in an Egyptian port city, with its upper section and a base plaque hidden. Within 10 minutes, identify the monument and the square it stands in, then describe the missing top section in enough detail to redraw it.
Rubric 1: The response identifies the monument as the “Greatness of Egypt” (Azamet Misr) statue, also called “Egypt, Mother of the World”, in Egypt Square, Port Said.
Rubric 2: The response describes the hidden top as a crowned woman in a long robe lifting a winged sun disc overhead with both arms.
Rubric 3: The response places the monument at the northern entrance of the Suez Canal in Al-Sharq district, Port Said.
Solution notes. Ground truth: the bronze “Greatness of Egypt” statue on Egypt Square by sculptor Essam Darwish. Hidden: the statue and a sponsor plaque naming the square. Clues left: granite base with bronze medallion and relief panel, waterfront apartment blocks, paving, Arabic signage fragment. Research path: (1) Search the visible clues, such as a winged sun disc on a granite base in an Egyptian port city, in Arabic and English. (2) Match the base and medallion to Arabic news coverage of the square. (3) Confirm name, location and design in independent sources. (4) Read the design description to recover the hidden top. Sources: Elbalad, Al-Watan, Dostor, Mobtada. Note: sources use several names and slightly different heights, so the rubric avoids measurements. Provenance: original photograph by Sagda El-Faiomy.

Instance 2: an ornamental gate pillar

Prompt: This photo shows an ornamental pillar beside a metal gate in an Egyptian city. The lettering on its top band has been hidden. Within 10 minutes, identify the city and the district where it stands, then state what the hidden lettering says.
Rubric 1: The response identifies the city as Port Said, Egypt.
Rubric 2: The response places the pillar on the Suez Canal waterfront in Al-Sharq district.
Rubric 3: The response states that the hidden lettering reads “PORT SAID” in capital letters.
Solution notes. Ground truth: an ornamental gate pillar on the Port Said waterfront, Al-Sharq district, photographed on site; the top band carries the city name in raised capitals. Hidden: that lettering. Clues left: carved bearded figure holding a torch, garlands and scrollwork, black iron gate, and a blue container or ship behind the fence. Research path: (1) Identify the royal-style ornament and search Arabic terms for decorative columns on the Port Said canal promenade. (2) Compare with Arabic coverage of the waterfront development. (3) Narrow to Al-Sharq district. (4) Infer the lettering from the gate's context. Sources: Masrawy and Youm7 coverage of Egypt Square and the canal promenade describe royal-style columns along the waterfront. Limit: no article I found documents this exact pillar, so the location rests on the photographer's own on-site record. Provenance: original photograph by Sagda El-Faiomy.

Instance 3: a historic waterfront building (licensed image)

Image credit: photo © Vyacheslav Argenberg, licensed CC BY 4.0, from Wikimedia Commons (“Port Said, Egypt, Suez Canal Authority”, uploaded 18 August 2021). Changes: parts hidden and cropped by me.
Prompt: This photo was taken from the water and shows a historic building on a canal bank in an Egyptian port city. Its central roof section is hidden. Within 10 minutes, identify the building and the authority that uses it, then describe the missing central section.
Rubric 1: The response identifies the building as the Suez Canal Authority building in Port Said, known locally as Al-Qubba (“the Dome”).
Rubric 2: The response states that the building sits on the west bank of the Suez Canal in Al-Sharq district, Port Said.
Rubric 3: The response describes the hidden section as a green dome on a raised central tower topped by a flagpole.
Solution notes. Ground truth: the Suez Canal Authority building (Al-Qubba), used to monitor ship traffic, built in the Islamic style with three green domes by the French contractor Edmond Coignet. Hidden: the central dome, tower and flag. Clues left: two green side domes, white arcaded facade, tugboats at the quay, and the canal waterfront. Research path: (1) Search the visible clues, such as three green domes and a waterfront palace in an Egyptian port city, in Arabic and English. (2) Match the facade and side domes to Arabic coverage of the building. (3) Confirm the owner, the district and the canal bank in independent sources. (4) Use photos and descriptions to recover the central dome and tower. Sources: Masrawy (15 May 2025), Youm7 (12 Dec 2024), Al Arabiya (4 Oct 2024), Watani (Jan 2018). Note: sources give 1895 for construction under Khedive Abbas Hilmi II, but one encyclopedia page says 1869, and the sources differ on who ordered it. The rubrics avoid dates and builders.
